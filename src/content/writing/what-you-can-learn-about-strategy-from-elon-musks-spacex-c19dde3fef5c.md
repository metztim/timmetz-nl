## #19 What You Can Learn About Strategy From Elon Musk’s SpaceX

*This series is about what I’m learning, observing and experiencing while building my startup* [*Saent*](http://saent.com) *(pronounce “Saint”). The good, the bad and especially the ugly, each time in under 500 words.*

![](/images/medium/b0e18a5be9d997ecb6440cee.jpg)

It takes quite a bit of “strategy” to get this rocket to liftoff.

When I mention strategy, terms like *vision*, *planning* and *pivot* might come to mind. You probably wouldn’t think of this:

> “I am going to wait for the next big thing.”

That’s what Steve Jobs answered when he was asked about his strategy for *Apple* in 1998. We now know that’s exactly what Jobs did; he did it with the *iPod*, and then again with the *iPhone*.

> “Good strategy almost always looks this simple and obvious and does not take a thick deck of PowerPoint slides to explain. […] Instead, a talented leader identifies the one or two critical issues in the situation and then focuses and concentrates action and resources on them.” *- Richard Rumelt in* [*Good Strategy / Bad Strategy*](http://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0307886239/ref=as_li_qf_sp_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&camp=1789&creative=9325&creativeASIN=0307886239&linkCode=as2&tag=doctodisco-20&linkId=DA7MS6O3L6XAMCW2)

## **Enter Elon Musk**

*Wait But Why* has written a rather extensive series on Elon Musk. The [first part](http://waitbutwhy.com/2015/05/elon-musk-the-worlds-raddest-man.html) is about how Musk is building *Tesla*, which is impressive to say the least. But then [the second half](http://waitbutwhy.com/2015/08/how-and-why-spacex-will-colonize-mars.html) explains what he does with the other 50% of his time: *SpaceX*.

![](/images/medium/25a0689e7557c4cb86cd9ed4.jpg)

Elon Musk is in the space business with SpaceX in order to save the human race (I’m not kidding).

*SpaceX* makes *Tesla*, *Apple*, Steve Jobs and whatever most other people on this planet are doing look insignificant.

**SpaceX wants to colonize Mars by sending 1,000,000 humans to live there within the next decades.**

Why do *that*?

**To save the human race from extinction, which will certainly happen if we just hang around here on earth forever.**

While this might sound crazy at first, reading through the *Wait But Why* articles actually makes it all sound logical and doable:

**In order to get a 1,000,000 people to Mars, we need to bring the travel cost per person down to $500,000.**

That’s it.

In these few sentences, I’ve explained you the why, what and how of *SpaceX*.

![](/images/medium/d218b4318f617e39b3e7e53f.webp)

The SpaceX Business Plan visualised, courtesy of [Wait But Why](http://waitbutwhy.com/2015/08/how-and-why-spacex-will-colonize-mars.html).

While reading the *Wait But Why* articles, the last thing on my mind was strategy. But once finished, something started to dawn on me: this is an amazing example of brilliant strategy hiding in plain sight.

Musk has taken on one of the most, if not *the* most, challenging project anyone could ever think of and has boiled it down to a simple plan. He has done that *so* well, you would almost forget how complex an undertaking this actually is:

- Why Mars and not some other planet?
- How the hell do you get people to Mars?
- How on earth can people live there? *(pun intended)*
- Why 1,000,000 people at a price of $500,000 per person?
- How do you even launch a rocket?

Stuff like that.

## **Back to planet earth**

Which brings us back to our own insignificant struggles and ambitions: I needed 3,000 words to explain [our crowdfunding strategy](/writing/were-planning-to-raise-100k-on-indiegogo-heres-our-entire-strategy-38ef0d197b74). And I’m currently writing an even lengthier business plan to prepare for our next funding round.

Makes you think, right?

Elon Musk needs a few sentences to explain how he’s going to save the human race. Steve Jobs needed just one sentence to sum up his revival strategy for *Apple*. Perhaps I should also be able to sum up Saent’s long-term plans a few paragraphs at most?
